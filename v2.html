The situation

Joined Kompas as PM for the payment ecosystem, promotion engine, loyalty programme, and e-learning platform — spanning multiple digital platforms and business units. The payment funnel was underperforming, the loyalty programme didn't exist yet, and the e-learning platform needed a growth push. I also acted as internal project coordinator for vendor-led deliveries across the business.

GoPay Recurring — the insight that mattered

When I looked at subscriber drop-off data, the pattern was clear: users weren't churning because they didn't want to pay — they were churning because they couldn't. Many didn't own a credit card. The solution wasn't a checkout redesign. It was adding GoPay Recurring as a payment method — meeting users where they already were financially. This single initiative directly addressed a payment accessibility gap that was quietly killing subscription retention.

"Most payment problems look like UX problems on the surface. This one was an access problem. The fix wasn't making the checkout prettier — it was making it possible for more people to actually complete it."
What I owned across the role
  • Built the payment monitoring framework from scratch — instrumented full funnel in GA4, Mixpanel, and PostHog, tracking 6 core metrics including Payment Success Rate, CVR of Purchased to Selected Payment, and Payment Method Selection Rate.
  • Shipped GoPay Recurring to solve subscriber payment accessibility — identified the gap, scoped the integration, coordinated with engineering and payment gateway teams.
  • Added personalized payment recommendations based on user history and most-used methods — reduced decision friction at checkout.
  • Ran monthly RCA cadences with engineering and commercial leads to systematically diagnose and address payment funnel issues.
  • Built Kompas.id's first transaction-based loyalty points programme from zero — defined earn/burn logic, drafted integration specs, facilitated alignment between conflicting finance and growth priorities.
  • Acting internal PIC for vendor-led deliveries: e-learning platform (Kompas Institute) and eBook feature enhancements — gathered cross-BU requirements, managed vendor timelines, signed off on scope at delivery milestones.

0→1 Marketplace 30-person Squad iOS · Android · Web Culture Building Founder Mode
The real story

I was 22, a fresh graduate, and the first PM at Toco. There was no roadmap, no process, no senior PM above me. The initial brief was a homepage revamp — but when the CEO saw the result, he realised the product needed to be rebuilt entirely from zero. That became my responsibility to lead.

What made it genuinely hard wasn't the feature complexity. It was that I had to build the product, the operations, and the ways of working all at the same time — with a team of 5, limited resources, and a business direction that kept evolving mid-sprint.

"There was a phase where I genuinely didn't know where to start — because everything felt urgent and everything was connected. What I learned is that in a zero-to-one environment, your job isn't to have all the answers. It's to make sure the team can keep moving even when nothing is certain."
How I actually built it
  • Started with structure, not speed: Documented decisions even when they were tentative. Mapped dependencies between teams and features. Translated vague stakeholder needs into actionable specs before a single line of code was written.
  • Made the sequencing call: Prioritised seller activation before buyer acquisition — based on supply-side density hypotheses. This sequencing decision shaped every sprint for the first 5 months and was the foundation that made 1M+ MAU possible.
  • Shipped four platforms simultaneously: Led a 30-person squad (engineering, design, QA, ops) to ship iOS app, Android app, web platform, and CMS at the same time — with one shared engineering team.
  • Made the hard trade-off: Explicitly chose to delay web parity features to protect mobile app quality at launch. Mobile-first behaviour in early testing justified the call. It was the right one.
  • Built the team culture: Created 'TocoFun' — regular team dinners and activities outside work hours. Set up retros as the space where professional friction got resolved openly, not suppressed. The result: most of the core team stayed together for 2–3 years. At a startup. That's not an accident.
  • Handled everything else too: Vendor coordination, logistics partner outreach, third-party integrations, data dashboard setup, client meetings, go-to-market thinking, campaign execution — because in a small founding team, that's the job.
